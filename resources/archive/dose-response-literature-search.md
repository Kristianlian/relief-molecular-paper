# Volume Dose-Response Comparison Table (Low vs. Moderate)

## RELIEF (this study)

**Population:** Young (<30y) and old (>70y) adults; old passive control group. Bayesian multilevel models, contrasts expressed as posterior mean ratio (or Nm difference for peak torque), with 95% ETI and `pos` (directional probability).

**Exercise volume load:** Moderate ≈ 2.8× higher than low in both age groups (young mod: 137,439 ± 54,492 kg; young low: 50,694 ± 25,708 kg; old mod: 118,125 ± 38,225 kg; old low: 42,134 ± 14,352 kg).

| Outcome | Young: Low | Young: Mod | Young: Mod vs. Low | Old: Low | Old: Mod | Old: Mod vs. Low | Volume-dose × Age interaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thigh muscle volume** | +5.0% (pos >99.9%) | +7.0% (pos >99.9%) | Mod +1.9% larger (pos >99.9%) | +2.9% (pos =99.9%) | +4.6% (pos >99.9%) | Mod +1.6% larger (pos =99.9%) | No credible difference (pos =69.3%) — dose advantage consistent across ages |
| **Muscle thickness** | +8.9% (pos >99.9%) | +12.1% (pos >99.9%) | Mod +3% larger, uncertain (pos =84.3%) | +3.2%, uncertain (pos =90.7%) | +12.3% (pos >99.9%) | Mod +8.9% larger (pos =99.9%) | Moderate evidence dose effect **smaller in young** than old (pos =91.8% old>young) — opposite pattern to muscle volume |
| **Upper-body lean mass** | +3.3% (pos >99.9%) | +2.9% (pos =99.9%) | No difference (pos =37.4%) | +1.1%, uncertain (pos =89.1%) | +1.2%, uncertain (pos =89.9%) | No difference (pos =52.3%) | No credible difference (pos =39.0%) |
| **Peak torque (isometric/60/120/240°/s)** | Large increase all speeds | Large increase, slightly smaller | **Low > Mod** across all 4 speeds (pos =95.1–99.4%) | Large increase all speeds | Large increase all speeds | No difference, weak lean to low (pos =28–40%) | Direction of age effect flips with volume; neither strong |
| **1RM leg press** | +12.2% (pos >99.9%) | +13.4% (pos >99.9%) | Weak lean to Mod (pos =72.8%) | +13.3% (pos >99.9%) | +13.0% (pos >99.9%) | No difference (pos =44.1%) | Weak evidence dose effect **larger in young** (pos =71.2%) |
| **Bicep curl MVC** | +3.9% (pos =97.4%) | +1.0%, weak (pos =70.7%) | **Low > Mod** (pos =95.1%) | +2.0%, uncertain (pos =85.5%) | +4.0% (pos =98.0%) | **Mod > Low** (pos =90.6%) | Credible: dose effect direction **opposite between ages** (mean ratio =0.95, young smaller/reversed vs. old) |
| **UBF protein (wk3)** | +15.6% (pos =82.7%) | +18.2% (pos =81.9%) | No difference (pos =52.8%) | +28.7% (pos =90.7%) | +14.4% (pos =76.7%) | Weak lean to Low (pos =68.1% low>mod) | Weak lean dose effect larger in young (pos =65.6%), wide uncertainty |
| **c-Myc protein, high peak (wk3)** | +4.0%, weak (pos =53.7%) | +36.6% (pos =90.4%) | **Mod > Low** (pos =83.8%) | +8.7%, weak (pos =60.3%) | +48.8% (pos =95.3%) | **Mod > Low** (pos =86.2%) | No credible difference (pos =45.6%) — dose advantage consistent across ages |

### Key pattern for the dose-response narrative
- **Hypertrophy outcomes (muscle volume, thickness, c-Myc):** moderate > low, fairly consistently, in both age groups.
- **Functional/strength outcomes (peak torque, bicep curl MVC):** the dose-response reverses or flips direction — low > moderate in young for both peak torque and bicep MVC; old shows no difference (torque) or moderate > low (bicep MVC) — i.e., **the direction of the volume effect is not consistent across age for strength outcomes**, unlike the fairly uniform pattern for hypertrophy.
- **1RM leg press and upper-body lean mass:** little to no credible dose effect either direction in either age group — a "null zone" outcome pair.

### Cross-study pattern check
Nearly all of the general/non-RELIEF literature falls into a **"no dissociation"** bucket, where higher dose favors both hypertrophy and strength/function together, with no separation between the two: **Krieger 2009/2010** (multiple sets beat single sets for both hypertrophy and strength, in separate papers but the same direction), **Rocha 2023/2024** (HV-RT beat LV-RT for both upper- and lower-limb strength, with a trend favoring HV-RT for functional fitness too — though volume didn't move body composition either way), **Chen 2023** (MVRT outperformed MRT for both muscle mass and lower-extremity strength/physical performance), **Lixandrão 2024** (a genuine joint time × set interaction for both qCSA and 1RM in non-responders), and **Hammarström 2020** (moderate volume outperformed low volume for both CSA/fibre-type transitions and strength gains, in young adults specifically).

Only **one study** in this list shows anything closer to a partial split rather than outright no-dissociation: **Pelland 2024/2025**, the largest and most recent meta-regression, where volume helps both outcomes with 100% posterior probability, but strength shows considerably steeper diminishing returns than hypertrophy — the closest general-literature approximation of a partial split, though it still stops well short of an outright reversal, and its sample is young-skewed (mean age ~25y) rather than an age comparison.

**Schoenfeld 2017** doesn't fit either bucket cleanly, since it manipulates load rather than volume, and its pattern actually runs the opposite direction from "strength plateaus early" — hypertrophy was insensitive to load while strength was clearly load-dependent.

Notably, Hammarström's young-only sample still shows moderate favoring both outcomes, which strengthens the case that RELIEF's young-adult reversal (low > moderate for peak torque and bicep MVC) is unusual even within a young population alone, not just relative to older-adult literature. **Da Silva 2018 and Schoenfeld 2019 are the closest parallels to RELIEF's pattern** — genuine volume-based dissociations where more volume clearly helped hypertrophy but did not help (and to some extent didn't need to help) strength/neuromuscular outcomes. Schoenfeld 2019 is a particularly strong match: a three-arm RCT directly manipulating volume (1 vs. 3 vs. 5 sets) in resistance-trained young men, finding graded hypertrophy gains with volume but similar strength/endurance gains across all three volume conditions — extending the dissociation seen in Da Silva's older-adult sample to a young, trained population as well. Worth prioritizing both citations, and worth being explicit in your discussion that RELIEF's *directional reversal* is more pronounced than what Da Silva, Schoenfeld 2019, Pelland, or the general literature typically reports — none of the studies gathered so far show low-volume actually *outperforming* moderate/higher-volume for a strength or functional outcome, which is what RELIEF found for peak torque and bicep curl MVC in young adults specifically.

**Only two studies found with a true within-study young-vs-old dose manipulation** (both age groups randomized to a low/moderate or low/high dose within the same trial): Bickel 2011 and Létocart 2021. No other study matching RELIEF's exact design (young and old, both randomized across volume conditions) was located.
